# 01 — Statements and direct proofs

First part of curriculum **1c**: move from checking examples to proving a claim for every permitted input. This is one introductory lesson, not completion of the entire logic/proof topic.

## Prerequisites

Signed-integer arithmetic, substitution, and expansion such as $(2k+1)^2$. Check these before starting; repair **1a** as needed. No calculus or proof-assistant installation is required. Readiness is not yet established.

## Objectives

- Distinguish a proposition from an open sentence with a free variable.
- Name a claim's domain, hypothesis, conclusion, and quantifier.
- Use the definitions of even, odd, and divisibility with integer witnesses.
- Write a direct proof for an arbitrary input, without assuming its conclusion.
- Refute an incorrect universal claim or converse with one counterexample.
- Explain the difference between example checking and general proof.

## Exit test — 15 minutes, unaided

For every integer $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $5n+2$ is odd. Identify the domain, hypothesis, and conclusion. Prove the claim directly from the definition of odd, supply an explicit integer witness for the conclusion, and explain why three successful numerical examples would not prove the claim.

Use no notes, tutor, solutions, or computer. Pass only with a valid argument for arbitrary permitted $n$, a justified integer witness, and a correct explanation of why examples are insufficient. Record actual elapsed time and assistance; then supply your own dated 2–4 sentence reflection after grading.
