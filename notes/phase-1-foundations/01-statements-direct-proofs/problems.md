# Problems: statements and direct proofs

Problems **1–14 are required**. Problem **15 is optional**. Write domains and justify integer witnesses. Keep [the separate solutions](solutions.md) closed during first attempts and the assessment. Record assistance and corrections in learner work.

## Drill

1. Which are propositions: (a) $9$ is odd; (b) $x+1=4$ with $x$ unspecified; (c) for every integer $n$, $n+0=n$? Explain the open sentence.
2. For “for every integer $n$, if $6\mid n$, then $3\mid n$,” identify domain, hypothesis, and conclusion.
3. Supply integer witnesses showing that $-8$ is even, $-7$ is odd, and $3\mid(-12)$.
4. Prove directly that the sum of two even integers is even.
5. Prove directly that the product of an even integer and any integer is even.
6. For every integer $n$, prove that $4n+6$ is even.
7. For every integer $n$, prove that $6n+5$ is odd.

## Conceptual

8. A learner checks that $n^2+n+41$ is prime for $n=0,1,2$ and concludes it is prime for every nonnegative integer. Explain why the argument is invalid, then refute the claim using $n=41$. A prime is a positive integer greater than $1$ with only positive divisors $1$ and itself.
9. State the converse of “if an integer is divisible by $8$, then it is divisible by $4$.” Decide whether that converse holds and justify your decision.
10. A learner writes $a=2k+1$ and $b=2k+1$ to prove a claim for arbitrary odd integers $a,b$. Identify the extra assumption and repair the setup.

## Applied or structural

11. A device collects $n$ complete groups of $12$ readings, with $n$ a nonnegative integer. Prove the total can be split into groups of $3$ without leftovers. Identify the integer witness for divisibility.
12. A checkerboard row has an odd number $n$ of cells. A border adds one cell at each end. Prove the new row still has an odd number of cells.
13. Let $a,b$ be integers. Prove that if $3\mid a$ and $3\mid b$, then $3\mid(2a-b)$. Show the witness explicitly.
14. Prove that if integers $a,b$ are odd, then $ab$ is odd. Explain why the test $a=b=1$ is not a proof.

## Optional stretch

15. Prove: for every integer $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $8\mid(n^2-1)$. You may use the fact that every integer is either even or odd; show why the product of two consecutive integers is even.

## Exit test — 15 minutes, unaided

For every integer $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $5n+2$ is odd. Identify the domain, hypothesis, and conclusion. Prove the claim directly from the definition of odd, supply an explicit integer witness for the conclusion, and explain why three successful numerical examples would not prove the claim.

Use no notes, tutor, solutions, or computer. Pass only with a valid argument for arbitrary permitted $n$, a justified integer witness, and a correct explanation of why examples are insufficient. Record actual elapsed time and assistance; then supply your own dated 2–4 sentence reflection after grading.
