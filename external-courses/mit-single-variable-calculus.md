## Open Learning Library practice

### 18.01.1x · Calculus 1A · Differentiation

**Edition** · 2T2019. Use alongside the existing 18.01 rate-of-change work. [About](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+18.01.1x+2T2019/about) · [Course outline](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+18.01.1x+2T2019/course/)

- **First checkpoint · 25 minutes.** Open **Unit 1: The Derivative → What is the derivative?** Compute two secant slopes for a simple position function at progressively smaller intervals and explain their relation to instantaneous velocity. Use Unit 0 limits and continuity if needed.
- **Later sequence.** Follow Units 2–4 through derivative rules, approximation and applications. Check a linear approximation against the original function, then justify a maximum or minimum by checking critical points and the domain boundary. Use the published exam preparation after practice.

### 18.01.2x · Calculus 1B · Integration

**Edition** · 3T2019. Start after Part 1's derivative rules and slope interpretation. [About](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+18.01.2x+3T2019/about) · [Course outline](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+18.01.2x+3T2019/course/)

- **First checkpoint · 25 minutes.** Open **Unit 1 → Differentials and antiderivatives** and check one antiderivative by differentiating it. Use **Unit 2 → The definite integral** next. Compare a small rectangle sum for a velocity curve with the signed displacement from integration.
- **Later sequence.** Work through both Fundamental Theorem lessons in Unit 2, then select areas, average value, probability or numerical integration from Unit 3. Continue to techniques in Unit 4, including integration by parts.

### 18.01.3x · Calculus 1C · Coordinate Systems & Infinite Series

**Edition** · 1T2020. Bring differentiation and integration from Parts 1–2. [About](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+18.01.3x+1T2020/about) · [Course outline](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/courses/course-v1:MITx+18.01.3x+1T2020/course/)

- **First checkpoint · 25 minutes.** Open **Unit 1: Coordinate systems → Parametric Curves**. Choose a circle parameterization, compute position and speed at one parameter value, and verify the point lies on the circle. Record the last completed item.
- **Later sequence.** Continue to polar coordinates, then use Unit 2 for improper integrals, convergence and Taylor series. Compare a Taylor approximation with the original function at two distances from the expansion point and state the range where it meets a declared error tolerance.
